For example, the Schrödinger wavefunction for a pair of particles, \( \Psi(x_1, x_2) \), cannot generally be written in the form \( \Psi = \Phi(x_1) \Phi(x_2) \), but only in the form \( \Psi = \sum_{i,j} \Phi(x_1) \Phi(x_2) \Phi(x_1) \Phi(x_2) \). In the latter case there is no single state (wave function) for particle 1 alone or particle 2 alone, but only the superposition of such cases. In fact to any arbitrary choice of state for one subsystem there will correspond a (relative) state for the other, which will give a single superposition of a subsystem and not a superposition of a subsystem and a subsystem. Such correlations between systems arise from interaction of the systems, and from our point of view all measurement and observation processes are to be regarded simply as interactions between the observer, or measuring apparatus, and objective system, which produce strong correlations.
