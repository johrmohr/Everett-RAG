Dear wheelers:  

Two years ago the Everett family enjoyed our first visit to Texas with you. One year ago I attempted to convey our gratitude for this visit in a letter never sent, but then we saw you at the Misner's upon receiving your honorary degree at U. of Md.  

Now we are writing to say how much we enjoyed the PSS Einstein show, March 13, hosted by you, Peter U., and the Texas U. Physics Dept. Everyone should be most pleased with how it came out and we hope it will be aired on commercial television in the future in order to reach more viewers. Everyone should see it! We thought the models and experiments were excellent as was the manner of presentation as though the viewer were being taken on an informal, personal tour. Hugh wants to know if this marks the start of your new career in show business?  

There are two things about Hugh that perhaps need clearing up. This is why the meeting at Austin two years ago with Bryce DeWitt, the young Britishers, and others was such a great thing for him to participate in.  

Secondly, in line with the above thought, Hugh's refusal to comment on other's papers in same area of study is not necessarily instigated by contrarieness, but because in most cases the intricate subject matter is not so easily dispensed with. He is happy to meet with such persons when mutually convenient. Indeed, Hugh has been known to even try to

Dear Wheelers:  

Just a little late with our holiday season greeting.  

Since we last forwarded some material to you (last summarized) have been wanting to correct what probably appeared to be an indication of my part at least at the recent flurry of activity over renewed interest in Hugh's Princeton thesis topic.  

At the time, it was not clear to me why Hugh just couldn't write some quick acknowledgements to some of the mail, just as a matter of courtesy. In retrospect I better understand that the subject matter is not so easily dispensed with, and that while he is more than willing to sit down and talk with whomever on points of agreement/disagreement, etc., that it is most difficult to carry on a one- sided discourse via mail. One fellow, Paul Benioff, I think, Hugh even tried to contact by phone, but it was near Thanksgiving vacation, and I don't believe they ever did get together.  

This is all an explanation for my rather cusory correspondence which I fear sounded much more than as though Hugh was not concerned. and I were exactly and I were exactly I felt I was not wording what Hugh's real (f) thoughts were as (sequently) as he can express himself, but I felt some explanation was due you and the letters forwarded to us by you.  

So, this is to say, I understand Hugh's reluctance to discuss this topic other than in person, and it is not because of unconcern. Indeed, he seems quite pleased that he is now receiving a small measure of recognition. One fellow recently sent a page from his book on Human Values (enclosed) referring to Hugh Everett's thesis as "almost as a fitting tribute to Einstein," which indeed will be fun to read in a book!  

I felt I was not expressing Hugh's actual thoughts on the matter but didn't have too much of an inkling as to what they were. This is to assure you that we are most appreciative of everything you've done regarding the expediting the flow and exchange of ideas. The Austin get- together was really great. Also, you may have started something going with our daughter Liz and her friend in the Army she visited in Austin. She and Mark visited him in Hawaii in February, and now she is seriously considering visiting him on a more permanent basis in June!  

My letter writing hasn't improved much, but this will have to do.  

Hugh's Travel Agency has opened for business, so you can see, he is a man of several interests! We note that John Toll may become U. of Mi. president. And I think Benedict's graduates this year.  

Have a good summer in Maine.  

Sincerely,  

Nancy and Hugh Everett

---
