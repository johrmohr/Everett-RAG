We have here only a special case of the following general principle which will hold for any situation which is treated entirely wave mechanically:  

Principle: For any situation in which the existence of a property \(R_{i}\) for a subsystem \(S_{i}\) of a composite system \(S\) will imply the later property \(Q_{i}\) for \(S\) , then it is also true that an initial state for \(S_{i}\) of the form \(\psi_{i} = \sum_{i} a_{i} \psi_{i}\) , which is a superposition.

That is, for any arrangement of an interaction between two systems \(S_{and} S_2\), which has the property that each initial state \(\psi_i^S \psi_i^{S_2}\) will result in a final situation with total state \(\psi_i^{S_1 + S_2}\), an initial state of \(S_1\) of the form \(\sum_i a_i \psi_i^{S_1}\) will lead, after interaction, to the superposition \(\sum_i a_i \psi_i^{S_1 + S_2}\) for the whole system. 

This follows immediately from the superposition principle for solutions of a linear wave equation. It therefore holds for any system of quantum mechanics for which the superposition principle holds, both particle and field theories, relativistic or not, and is applicable to all physical systems, regardless of size.
