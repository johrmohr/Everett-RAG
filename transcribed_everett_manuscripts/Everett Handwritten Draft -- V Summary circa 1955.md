2. The compound interpretation: This is the position interpretation developed by Bohr. The function is not regarded as an objective description of a physical system, i.e., it is in no sense a computational model, but is regarded as a mere abstractive which enables one to make statistical predictions, albeit the best prediction that it is possible to make. This interpretation itself drives the very possibility of conceptual models applicable to the quantum realm, but asserts that the totality of phenomena can only be understood by the use of different, mutually exclusive (i.e., complementary) models in different situations. All statements about atomic phenomena are regarded as meaningless unless accompanied by a complete description (a classical level) of an experimental arrangement. 

While undoubtedly safe from contradiction, due to its extreme construction, it is perhaps uncertain, we do not believe it to be the primary purpose of any theoretical physics. It is more content to accept "theories of the world" as a kind of conception at a more cost in the applicability of their concepts which is a sterile occupation, but to make useful models which serve for a time and are replaced as they are outworn. 

Another objectionable feature of this position is its strong reliance upon the classical level which precludes any possibility of explaining this level in the basis of an underlying quantum theory.
