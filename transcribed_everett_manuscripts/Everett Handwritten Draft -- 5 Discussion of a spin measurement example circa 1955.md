There is another case however, there
usually encountered in fact, when the transition
probability concept is somewhat more justified. This
is the case when the perturbation is due to initiation
of the system (S) with another system S2, and
not simply a time dependence of S1's Hamiltonian
as in the case just considered. In this situation
the interaction produces a composite system state
for which there are no general no independent
subsystems states. However, we have seen,
for purpose of calculating expectations operators
on S1 alone we can regard S1 as being represented
by a certain mixture. Accordingly to this picture the
states of subsystem S1 are gradually converted into
mixtures by the interaction with S2 and the concept
of transition probability makes some sense. Of course
it must be remembered that this picture is only justified
to long as further measurements on S1 alone are
contemplated, and any attempt to make simultaneous
determination in S1 and S2 involves the composite state
where interference properties may be important.

An example is a hydrogen atom interacting with
the electromagnetic field. After a time of interaction we
can picture the atom as being in a mixture of its
states, no long as we consider any future measurement
on the atom. But actually the state of
the atom is dependent upon (correlated with) the
state of the field, and some process involving both
atom and field could conceivably depend on interference
effects between the states of the particles aligned mixture.
Via time interactions, however, the concept of transition
probability is quite useful and justified.
