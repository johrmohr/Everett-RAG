## §4. Approximate Measurement 

A phenomenon which is difficult to understand within the framework of the probabilistic interpretation of quantum mechanics is the result of an approximate measurement. In the abstract formulation of the causal theory there are two fundamental processes, the deterministic, probabilistic process 1, corresponding to precise measurement) and the continuous, deterministic process 2, corresponding to the sense of any measurement. What mimics of probability and causality are we to apply to the case where only an approximate measurement is effected (i.e., when the apparatus or observer initiates only exactly and for a finite time with the optical system)? 

In the case of approximate measurement we need to be supplied with rules which will tell us, for any initial object system state, first with what probability can we expect the various particles appearing readings, and second what will we plot to arrive to the system after the value has been observed. We shall see that it is generally impossible to give these rules within a framework which considers the apparatus or observer as performing an (abstract) observation subject to process 1, and that it is necessary, in order to give a full account of approximate measurement, to treat the entire system including apparatus or observer, wave-mechanically.
