Dear Hugh, - & had hoped to write up the notes & took in Copenhagen, but they are so incomplete and the job is so tough that & hope you will pardon me for sending them along as & took them. Here are comments: 

(1) much of what is said in objection to your work is quite irrelevant. 

(2) much is relevant: The difficulty of expressing in everyday words the going on in a mathematical scheme that is about as far removed as it can be from the everyday description. The contradictions of misunderstanding that will arise; the very very heavy burden and responsibility you have to state everything in such a way that these misunderstandings can arise. 

(3) this appallingly difficult job & feel (and only with the ability in thinking you have the ability in thinking and writing to accomplish, but it is going to take a lot of time, a lot of heavy argument with a practical touch mixed with a lot of writing & rewriting.

---

(4) The combination of qualities, to accept connections in a humble spirit, but to insist on the soundness of certain fundamental principles, is one that is rare but indispensable; & that you have it. But it won't do much good unless you go and fight with the greatest fighters. Frankly, & feel about 2 more months now, & while day by day argument are needed to get the things out of the woods, not out of the formation. 

(5) Regarding your own, & have had to write the customary annual letter of assessment to the National Union Association Fellowship Board, and in speaking of the need for you to go to Copenhagen, & concluded with this sentence: " & feel Ernest's very original work is destined to become widely known, and it ought to have the big sound out of it before it is published rather than after." 

(6) As in my earlier letter, & had to my original offer to pay one half of your minimum note one way between you & Copenhagen.

---

(Canono Lunea, Mr. Gabriel Reine, 45 W. 45th St. N. Y. 36, Circle 5-7711) can get your passage when no one else can) 

(7) & also fee that the Dennis Academy and under Dennis auspices is the best possible place for you to publish your work: a full length presentation, going to a wide audience 

(8) Page Etienne was most helpful. But he had a tendency to insist that small illustrations were too small e²/πc, was essential for a world in which one cannot be more normal words. On the contrary, & argued that the words came first - it could have small or large e²/πc, but great only complicated systems, and solutions and you have systems that must find a way to communicate with each other to give mutual assistance in the struggle for existence; in the struggle for survival words would necessarily be invented to deal with a large e²/πc. You don't print give a list of words and then ask what systems are compatible with them; instead, the system comes first, and the words second.

---

(9) My envelope notes are fantastically incomplete because I wrote almost never when I was with Bob, only with Amy Peterson.
